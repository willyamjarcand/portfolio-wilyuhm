# On Side Projects

I have a graveyard of half-finished side projects. I'm not ashamed of it anymore.

## The Graveyard is Fine

Every abandoned project taught me something. The Rust CLI I gave up on taught me enough about ownership that it made me a better systems thinker. The mobile app I stopped at the MVP screen taught me I don't enjoy mobile development. These are useful data points.

## What Actually Ships

Looking back at the projects I've actually finished, they share a few traits:

- **Small scope from the start.** Not "I'll add more later" small — genuinely small.
- **Personal itch.** I built them because I was annoyed something didn't exist, not because it seemed like a good portfolio piece.
- **Fun technology.** If the stack is boring to me, I'll drop it the moment it gets hard.

## The Portfolio Trap

Building a portfolio project *as* a portfolio project is a trap. You're optimizing for how it looks to a recruiter, not for whether you'll actually finish it. The best portfolio projects are the ones you built for yourself.
