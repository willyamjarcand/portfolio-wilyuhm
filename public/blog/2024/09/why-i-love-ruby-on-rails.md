# Why I Still Love Ruby on Rails

The Rails-is-dead takes come in waves. They're wrong.

## Convention over configuration

Rails made a bet in 2004 that most web apps need the same things: a database, a router, templates, a mailer. It baked in opinions about all of it. The bet paid off.

When I start a new Rails app I'm productive in minutes. There's no decision fatigue about folder structure, ORM, or how to run background jobs. That headspace goes into the actual problem.

## The "magic" is just conventions

New developers sometimes get spooked by Rails magic — `before_action`, autoloading, `has_many`. It's not magic. It's conventions that become second nature in a week. After that, you stop thinking about them entirely, which is the point.

## When to use something else

Rails is not the answer for everything. Real-time systems, ML pipelines, CLI tools — there are better fits. But for a CRUD web app with a team of one to ten, Rails is still hard to beat.

## The ecosystem

The gem ecosystem is mature. Devise, Sidekiq, Pundit, ActiveStorage — these are battle-tested and well-documented. You're not evaluating five competing authentication libraries. You just use Devise.

I'll keep using Rails until something better comes along. Nothing has yet.
