# Learning in Public

The best thing I did for my growth as an engineer was start sharing what I was learning, even when — especially when — I didn't fully understand it yet.

## The fear

The obvious objection: what if I'm wrong? What if someone more experienced sees my post and it has a mistake in it?

They will. It's fine. A public correction is one of the fastest ways to learn. The embarrassment fades in a day. The correct mental model stays.

## What "public" means

It doesn't have to be a blog post with thousands of readers. It can be:

- A tweet about something you just figured out
- A note in a shared team doc
- A comment on a GitHub issue

The audience doesn't have to be large. The act of externalizing your thinking is what sharpens it.

## The rubber duck effect, scaled

When you write for an audience — even an imaginary one — you have to explain things completely. You can't hand-wave. The gaps in your understanding become obvious. That's the value.

## Start small

You don't need a perfect blog. Write one thing. Put it somewhere. See what happens.
